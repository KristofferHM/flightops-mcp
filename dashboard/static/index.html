                How to read this board

                A colour describes the weather category at that airport, never the airport's status. A red diamond is low cloud or poor visibility, not a closed airport, and a green circle is not a promise that anything is running.

                Coverage

                The airport list is worldwide: the 3,270 large and medium airports with scheduled service in OurAirports. Live traffic is not. OpenSky's receivers are dense over Europe and North America and sparse over Africa, South America and much of Asia, so an empty patch of sky means no receiver heard anything there, not that no aircraft are flying.

                Aircraft: what is observed, what is estimated, what has no source

                A transponder broadcasts where an aircraft is, not where it is going. So the inspector keeps three lines apart. Transit is observed: the path OpenSky recorded, each point with its own time, never drawn past the last one. Estimated arrival is an inference: OpenSky's guess from the first and last positions it heard, available once a flight has ended, and never a schedule. Destination has no source: it needs a schedule provider, and none is configured. The airline is derived from the callsign's first three letters through a bundled table; a code the table does not hold is shown bare.

                The chips above the map sort the sky by who is flying, from the callsign alone: OpenSky sends no aircraft type, and the note under the chips says so. The inspector names the rule that decided. A business jet with an airline-shaped callsign reads as an airline, a helicopter under its registration as general aviation, and a designator the tables do not hold stays unknown rather than being guessed. The counts describe the whole region; a chip changes what is drawn, never what is fetched.

                Choose what is drawn by sorting first and filtering second. The cap draws at most that many, the same ones every time for the same data, and says how many it left out. Focusing an aircraft dims the rest, draws the positions heard since this page opened, and asks OpenSky for its transit (4 credits). Follow keeps it centred as new positions arrive; it lets go on its own when the aircraft goes stale or leaves the snapshot, and says which. Escape releases it.

                What traffic costs

                Each refresh of the traffic layer costs 4 OpenSky credits, for any region. The layer refreshes no faster than its hourly ceiling allows, and stops with the reason on the map when the ceiling is reached. A triage costs up to 9 credits.
